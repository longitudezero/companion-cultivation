# Guild setting and task voice

> **ACTIVE · APPROVED NARRATIVE DIRECTION · 29 October 2026.** Agreed with the project owner. This document establishes the setting and writing rules for future task content; it does not claim that quests, characters, or narrative UI have been implemented. Individual task records live in the [main](../working/main-tasks.md) and [side](../working/side-tasks.md) rosters; unconfirmed quantities, rewards, and availability remain open.

## The player's job

The player is the newly hired botanist for an Adventurers' Guild. The guild is exploring a dungeon, and its adventurers keep needing more herbs: for healing, food, alchemy, and other increasingly specific requests.

The garden is the player's work. Dungeon exploration, arena tournaments, and laboratory breakthroughs happen offscreen and provide reasons for botanical tasks. The player supports those activities through cultivation rather than becoming the adventuring hero.

As the garden develops, the requests become more varied and demanding. The guild's growing dependence on its gardening department is a recurring source of comedy.

## The instructor

An experienced guild botanist teaches the player. The instructor is knowledgeable, supportive, and direct, with a little sarcasm and very little filter. They want the player to succeed and do not mince words about a dying Plant.

Their frustration is with the situation or the guild's latest disaster. They remain on the player's side; the humor should never become cruelty toward a beginner.

## Stories through tasks

**Main tasks** tell a small, loosely connected story about the guild's ongoing work. A huge minotaur can create an urgent need for healing ingredients; progress in teleportation research can create unusual alchemical orders. These events give context to discoveries, Leaf deliveries, and family-unlock projects without requiring a separate adventure system.

**Side tasks** are personal favors or smaller requests from individual guild members. Examples include an arena fighter seeking a performance booster or a nervous adventurer asking for a flower arrangement for someone they like. Recurring requesters are welcome, but elaborate character histories are unnecessary.

Storytelling happens primarily through the Taskboard's descriptions and requests. Cutscenes, playable dungeon exploration, and an elaborate dialogue system are not required by this direction.

## Voice and clarity

| Text | Speaker or role | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tutorial descriptions | The instructor | Helpful, conversational, funny, occasionally panicked |
| Main-task descriptions | The instructor reporting guild events | Small stories and escalating guild chaos |
| Side-task descriptions | The individual requester | Personal and eccentric, suited to that member |
| Objectives and progress | Neutral game interface | Short, precise, and unambiguous |

- Keep the required action clear even when the description is absurd. Players should never need to decipher a joke to understand an objective.
- Tutorial humor supports the lesson. Discovery descriptions can be curious and mysterious, production requests slightly absurd, and milestones funny while recognizing the player's accomplishment.
- Ordinary discovery tasks give hints rather than hidden recipes. The fully guided first transformation remains the deliberate exception described in the progression framework.
- Use the current species names and approved mechanics when writing task text. A narrative request for potions or food does not itself add a potion-crafting or cooking system; the objective names the actual resources or actions required.

## Example request ideas

These are tone references, not approved entries in the task roster:

- **A Minor Minotaur Problem:** the expedition found the next dungeon floor; the minotaur found the expedition first. The infirmary needs ingredients.
- **A Major Breakthrough in Teleportation:** the alchemists have moved something across the laboratory and created several new problems. They need more samples.
- **A Completely Legal Advantage:** an arena fighter wants help preparing for a tournament and is being suspiciously specific about the desired benefits.
- **Love Is in the Air:** an adventurer needs a flower arrangement and has no idea where to start.

The examples above still need task records, requesters' names, delivery quantities, rewards, and family-unlock assignments. The separate main roster records the agreed opening sequence.
