# Field Manual page architecture

> **WORKING PAGE PLAN · 16 November 2026.** This document maps the approved first-draft manuscript into a book structure. It does not implement the Field Manual or alter the Cultivation Grimoire. The first in-game pass should follow this plan, then be reviewed at the normal play size before density or page count is treated as final.

## Outcome

The Field Manual uses five tabs and fifteen spreads, for thirty printed pages.

| Tab | Spreads | Printed pages | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Garden | 3 | 1–6 | Introduce the manual, the hex garden, and environmental Conditions |
| Effects | 3 | 7–12 | Explain Influences, cultivation Effects, propagation, and Blockers |
| Cultivation | 5 | 13–22 | Explain planting, time, Plants, Fungi, Mycelial ground, Transformations, and discovery |
| Guild | 2 | 23–26 | Explain Inventory, Garden Tools, commerce, tasks, rewards, and expansion |
| Controls | 2 | 27–30 | Record verified garden, book, preference, and save operations |

This is large enough to keep body text comfortable and diagrams useful, but still much smaller and more stable than the species catalogue in the Grimoire.

## Physical and visual direction

- Reuse the existing Grimoire tabletop, open-book geometry, paper, typography, page turns, stationery, notes, markers, page numbers, close ribbon, view controls, and tab construction.
- Give the Field Manual a blue leather cover. Keep the existing metal fittings so the two books read as a matching set.
- Reuse five existing physical tab styles in the order listed above. Their labels change, but their illustrated material treatment does not need to be redrawn.
- Use the highly ornamented chapter border only for the opening spread. Instructional spreads use the subdued page ornament so diagrams and prose retain the full reading width.
- Aim for no vertical scrolling at the book's normal reading size. Existing page scrolling remains a fallback for small windows and unusually large text.
- Use the existing Georgia-led typography and warm paper palette. Blue ink, blue-green rules, and restrained blue marginal ornaments can identify the Field Manual without recolouring every page.
- Prefer annotated illustrations, ledgers, ladders, and small marginal notes over interface cards. The result should resemble an illustrated handbook, not a settings screen.
- Reuse gameplay icons whenever they already carry meaning: Conditions, cultivation Effects, Plants, Fungi, Garden Tools, Blockers, Seeds, Spores, Leaves, Gold, and task rewards.
- Avoid full species illustrations except where a generic Plant or Fungus silhouette is needed. The manual teaches systems; the Grimoire remains the place for specimens.

## Tab order and identity

### Garden

The broad entry point. It teaches the visual language that the rest of the manual assumes.

Suggested identity: green-blue tab, hex or three-leaf symbol.

### Effects

The spatial-rules section. It groups everything that travels, combines, or is stopped.

Suggested identity: violet or deep teal tab, radiating-arrow symbol.

### Cultivation

The living-systems section. It follows a planted attempt through germination, lifecycle, harvest, fungal support, and discovery.

Suggested identity: warm ochre or rust tab, sprout symbol.

### Guild

The ownership and progression section. It covers possessions, commerce, requests, and expansion.

Suggested identity: burgundy or gold tab, satchel or guild-seal symbol.

### Controls

The operational reference. It holds interactions, display choices, book handling, and recovery.

Suggested identity: pale blue or parchment tab, compass or hand symbol.

The symbols can use simple existing glyphs in the first pass. New tab art is not required before the layout has been reviewed.

## Spread map

### Spread 1 · Field Manual and contents · pages 1–2

**Tab:** Garden  
**Purpose:** Establish the book's role and provide book-like front matter.

**Left page — Field Manual**

- Title: **Field Manual**.
- Short subtitle: a practical guide to tending the hex garden.
- One restrained emblem or launcher illustration centred like a frontispiece.
- The opening explanation: this book records shared rules; the garden and Inspector show the present; the Grimoire records species and discoveries.
- A short warning at the foot of the page: opening books, the Shop, Taskboard, Inventory, or main menu does not pause the garden.

**Right page — Contents**

- Five chapter entries with their page ranges.
- One-sentence description under each entry.
- Page references are printed text in the first pass, not interactive links.
- A small note that all chapters are available from the beginning.

**Visual treatment**

- This is the only spread that uses the full ornate chapter border.
- Keep generous empty paper around the frontispiece.
- Contents should look typeset rather than like five buttons.

### Spread 2 · The garden and its hexes · pages 3–4

**Tab:** Garden  
**Purpose:** Give the player the basic mental model and teach the hex's visual language.

**Left page — The garden at a glance**

- One occupant per hex: Plant, Fungus, temporary Garden Tool, Full Hex Blocker, or concealed planting attempt.
- Uncommitted proposals reserve a hex without becoming active.
- Edge Blockers occupy shared borders.
- Placement and open routes matter.
- Removing a source ends its continuing contribution but does not undo elapsed results.
- A small “live garden” marginal reminder that time continues until Pause is selected.

**Right page — Reading a hex**

- One large annotated hex diagram.
- Callouts for terrain fill, border, Illumination medallion, occupant artwork, lifecycle bar, Mycelial overlay, Blockers, planting markers, and selection outline.
- A short paragraph explaining that the Inspector gives the exact current state.
- A small comparison between full illustrations in the Inspector/Grimoire and compact gameplay icons elsewhere.

**Visual treatment**

- Use a real neutral hex from the game as the diagram base.
- Callout labels follow the outside of the hex like a botanical plate. Avoid boxed legends when a leader line is sufficient.

### Spread 3 · Conditions and neighbouring extremes · pages 5–6

**Tab:** Garden  
**Purpose:** Explain the three environmental ladders and how the resolved state is formed.

**Left page — Three environmental Properties**

- Moisture ladder: Dry → Damp → Flooded.
- Temperature ladder: Freezing → Cold → Temperate → Hot → Scorching.
- Illumination ladder: Dark → Dim → Lit → Bright.
- Show where each Property appears on the hex.
- Define baseline and resolved Condition.
- State that active Influences change the resolved Condition without rewriting the baseline.
- List Wetting, Drying, Warming, Cooling, Brightening, and Dimming beside their movement direction.

**Right page — Combining and neighbouring**

- Opposing Influences can cancel; results stop at the ends of a ladder.
- One emission applies once to a reached hex; separate sources can combine.
- Compact adjacency reference for Flooded, Scorching, Freezing, Bright, and Dark neighbours.
- Explain the one-step, non-chaining rule and cancellation between opposing extremes.
- Distinguish Flooded adjacency from Waterborne propagation.

**Visual treatment**

- Render the three ladders as illustrated horizontal scales, not form controls.
- Use a three-hex vignette for adjacency: extreme neighbour, affected middle hex, unaffected next hex.

### Spread 4 · Influences and cultivation Effects · pages 7–8

**Tab:** Effects  
**Purpose:** Establish the vocabulary used by later propagation and cultivation pages.

**Left page — Different kinds of change**

- Define Environmental Influence.
- Define Cultivation Effect.
- Define observation.
- Explain that environmental and cultivation footprints from the same Fungus can differ.
- Explain that an Effect can reach a Plant without being active in its present lifecycle window.
- Use the existing environmental and cultivation icons as a paired visual comparison.

**Right page — Cultivation Effect reference**

- Compact two-column reference for Growth speed, Ripening speed, germination chance, Leaf production, Seed production, harvest window, peak Leaf timing, Seed timing, extra Leaves, and extra Seeds.
- Short notes underneath: Growth and Ripening are separate; timing does not create products; Effects apply prospectively; positive and negative Effects can combine.

**Visual treatment**

- Right page uses small effect icons and short entries in two vertical columns.
- Do not reproduce the Inspector's card grid. The page should look like a labelled field guide plate.

### Spread 5 · Propagation and routes · pages 9–10

**Tab:** Effects  
**Purpose:** Explain how an Influence or Effect reaches its destinations.

**Left page — Range and directional Budget**

- Range includes the source hex and spreads through adjacent open hexes up to its distance.
- Alternate routes can go around an obstruction when Range remains.
- Directional propagation uses one of six headings.
- A directional route travels forward, then may turn or divide around obstruction.
- Every branch shares one Budget; branches do not receive fresh budgets.
- Directional Fungi keep their planted heading; directional Garden Tools can be rotated later.

**Right page — Airborne and Waterborne**

- Airborne follows ordinary open routes using Range or Budget.
- Waterborne enters Flooded ground through its initial footprint, then follows the connected Flooded network.
- Dry and Damp gaps break the network.
- A Blocker can divide a network, but another flooded route can go around it.
- Waterborne effects do not create their own canals.
- “Waterborne” describes delivery, not Moisture or Mycelial ground.

**Visual treatment**

- Four small diagrams: Range around a wall, shared directional branching, Airborne crossing ordinary ground, and Waterborne following a canal.
- The same source and destination positions should be used where possible so the propagation rules are easy to compare.
- Dotted arrows show attempted paths; solid arrows show successful paths.

### Spread 6 · Blockers · pages 11–12

**Tab:** Effects  
**Purpose:** Explain placement and provide the requested complete filter table.

**Left page — Controlling a route**

- Compare Edge and Full Hex Blockers with one diagram each.
- Edge forms stop matching crossings in both directions.
- Full Hex forms stop matching travel through the tile and matching adjacency across its sides.
- Any matching filter is enough to stop an effect.
- Blockers change ongoing and future reach; they do not undo earned results.
- Routes may go around them.
- Player Blockers are reusable and can be moved directly.

**Right page — Blocker filter reference**

- Print all nine Blocker designs and what each stops.
- Use the gameplay Blocker icons beside their names.
- Keep the wording identical to the approved manuscript.
- Finish with one line directing artwork, forms, and unlocked collections to the Grimoire's Blockers chapter.

**Visual treatment**

- The right page is a ruled ledger with nine rows, not nine cards.
- Use alternating faint paper tones only if the table needs help tracking across the page.

### Spread 7 · Planting and garden time · pages 13–14

**Tab:** Cultivation  
**Purpose:** Explain how attempts are planned, committed, concealed, and advanced.

**Left page — Seeds, Spores, and staging**

- Seeds plant Plants; Spores plant Fungi; Leaves cannot be planted.
- Spending more improves germination chance up to certainty.
- Germination Effects change Plant Seed attempts, not Fungus Spore attempts.
- Mycelial-dependent Fungi require Mycelial ground when committed.
- Four-step staging sequence: choose, place, revise, Apply.
- Explain reserved stock, Undo, Cancel, and all-or-nothing Apply.
- Mention immediate planting mode without repeating the whole sequence.

**Right page — Concealed germination and time**

- Seeds or Spores are spent at commitment.
- Success and failure remain visually identical until reveal.
- A pending attempt occupies the hex but is not alive and emits nothing.
- Success creates a 0%-Growth organism; failure frees the hex; there is no refund.
- Pause, 0.5×, 1×, and 2× affect the entire time-driven garden.
- Speed changes real waiting time, not biological requirements.
- Direct actions still work while paused.
- There is no offline catch-up.

**Visual treatment**

- Left page uses a four-step planting sequence along a curling vine or row of seed packets.
- Right page shows the same concealed marker branching to success and failure only after the reveal point.
- Do not print germination duration or hidden chance formulas.

### Spread 8 · Plant lifecycle and harvest · pages 15–16

**Tab:** Cultivation  
**Purpose:** Explain Growth, Ripening, production, destructive harvest, and environmental hazards.

**Left page — Growth and Ripening**

- Illustrated 0–200% lifecycle line: Growth, Ripening, Mature.
- State that 100% is not a universal best-harvest signal.
- Different Plants have different Leaf and Seed timing.
- The lifecycle bar marks known windows; the Inspector shows the present harvest.
- Conditions, Mycelial ground, and Effects can change speed or output.
- Production is earned under the Conditions that existed while the Plant passed through its productive windows.

**Right page — Harvest and hazards**

- Named Leaves are distinct products.
- Seeds may belong to the same species, another species, several species, or none.
- Leaves can decline as Seeds rise; extra hourly output is banked separately.
- Harvesting collects current output and removes the Plant.
- The whole harvest must fit or the action is refused.
- Continuous hazards have separate clocks and reset independently when their trigger ends.
- Dormancy can stop progress without stopping an active danger.
- Death before harvest loses uncollected products.

**Visual treatment**

- Reuse the Inspector's metal-framed lifecycle language in a printed, horizontal illustration.
- Use generic Leaf and Seed icons over example windows without assigning them to a named species.
- Hazards appear as a small cautionary marginal section, not a large warning panel.

### Spread 9 · Fungi and affinity · pages 17–18

**Tab:** Cultivation  
**Purpose:** Explain the Fungus lifecycle and the meaning of Independent, Preferring, and Dependent.

**Left page — Living sources**

- Fungi are living sources that can change the environment, cultivate Plants, support experiments, and produce Spores.
- Illustrated 0–200% Growth and Sporulation line.
- Emissions begin immediately after successful germination and continue while the Fungus lives.
- Harvesting collects available Spores, removes the Fungus, and ends its emissions.
- Living Fungi cannot be stored or moved.
- Directional Fungi keep their planted heading.
- Mycelord produces Mycelial ground rather than an ordinary package.

**Right page — Mycelial affinity**

- Three illustrated entries: Independent, Preferring, Dependent.
- Affinity describes the Fungus's own hex, not the receiving Plant's hex.
- Dependent Fungi continue emitting during their grace period and recover if support returns in time.
- The receiving Plant does not need Mycelial ground.
- Mycelord is not dependent, but sustained Flooded ground is dangerous and disrupts support.

**Visual treatment**

- Use three generic Fungus silhouettes over plain, Mycelial, and threatened ground.
- Avoid named Fungus effect values or durations.

### Spread 10 · Mycelial ground · pages 19–20

**Tab:** Cultivation  
**Purpose:** Give this spatial system enough room to be understood without a dense paragraph.

**Left page — A supported colony**

- Mycelial is a separate living ground network.
- Mycelord establishes a colony.
- New ground spreads gradually through supported neighbouring Mycelial hexes.
- Several colonies can overlap; one surviving contribution is enough to keep a hex Mycelial.
- Affinity labels refer to the Fungus source hex.

**Right page — Flooding, severing, and retreat**

- Flooded hexes cannot be newly colonised and cannot carry support.
- Matching Blockers can stop spread and sever a route.
- Unsupported contributions stop expanding and retreat gradually.
- Reconnecting can rescue ground that remains; lost ground must regrow.
- Mycelial generally slows Plant development and ordinary production; exact species responses remain discoveries.
- Contrast Flooded ground carrying Waterborne effects with Flooded ground breaking Mycelial support.

**Visual treatment**

- Use one continuous before-and-after garden diagram across both pages: healthy colony on the left, flooded or blocked cut and retreat on the right.
- Distinguish supported, retreating, and lost ground with the game's actual overlay plus simple line styles.

### Spread 11 · Transformations and recorded knowledge · pages 21–22

**Tab:** Cultivation  
**Purpose:** Acknowledge Transformations without teaching their solutions, then explain what the Grimoire records.

**Left page — Transformations**

- A Transformation changes one living Plant into another.
- Its necessary circumstances must remain satisfied for some time.
- The new Plant begins at 0% Growth.
- Successfully witnessed routes are recorded in the Cultivation Grimoire.
- Fungi and Garden Tools do not transform.
- No ingredients, Conditions, named Fungi, sequences, timings, destinations, alternatives, or Inspector guidance appear here.

**Right page — The Cultivation Grimoire**

- The Grimoire records knowledge, not ownership.
- Hidden Plants remain behind stable runes until they legitimately appear.
- A legitimate first appearance reveals identity permanently.
- Preferences, hazards, fungal responses, unusual traits, and Transformation routes require real observations.
- Fungus pages, Plant pages, Garden Tools, and Blockers each hold their own kinds of record.
- Field notes and page markers are reusable and do not occupy Inventory.

**Visual treatment**

- Keep the Transformation page deliberately spacious and mysterious: one generic Plant silhouette changing into another across a restrained botanical divider.
- The Grimoire page can use a small green-book illustration opposite the Field Manual's blue identity.

### Spread 12 · Inventory and Garden Tools · pages 23–24

**Tab:** Guild  
**Purpose:** Explain how possessions occupy space and how temporary tools differ from living organisms.

**Left page — Inventory**

- One square stores one identity or one individually tracked Garden Tool.
- Species-specific Seeds, Spores, and Leaves are separate identities.
- Ordinary materials and identical Blockers stack; Edge and Full Hex forms remain separate.
- Temporary Garden Tools do not stack because their lifetime and heading are individual.
- Rearranging and filtering do not change ownership.
- New identities need a free square; additions to an existing stack do not.
- Harvests, purchases, and rewards validate capacity before committing.
- Discard is permanent and reserved planting stock must first be released.

**Right page — Garden Tools**

- Temporary environmental sources, not living Fungi.
- Occupy a hex and immediately apply their listed footprint.
- Lifetime passes only while deployed.
- Returning to Inventory pauses the remaining lifetime.
- Tools can move directly; directional tools can rotate; neither action resets lifetime.
- Expired tools disappear and free their hex.
- Retrieval is refused safely if Inventory has become full.
- Exact effects, footprints, lifetimes, art, and collections remain in the Grimoire catalogue.

**Visual treatment**

- Left page resembles an illustrated satchel ledger with a few example stacks, never a screenshot of the Inventory grid.
- Right page shows a tool moving from bag to hex and back, with a small paused-hourglass cue.

### Spread 13 · Shop, tasks, and expansion · pages 25–26

**Tab:** Guild  
**Purpose:** Collect the Guild-facing progression rules in one place.

**Left page — Shop and expansion**

- Gold is Shop currency.
- Named Plant Leaves can be sold; Seeds, Spores, Blockers, Garden Tools, and trophies cannot.
- Beanstalk Seeds remain free as a recovery route.
- The Shop displays current prices, stock, quantities, and ownership.
- Transactions are atomic.
- Task rewards can unlock stock, land, Inventory space, and stationery.
- Land upgrades add rows or columns while preserving the existing layout.
- Inventory upgrades add usable squares without moving stacks.
- Exact prices and limits stay in the Shop.

**Right page — Tasks and rewards**

- Main, Side, and Completed groups.
- Objectives make a task Ready; rewards arrive only when it is explicitly completed.
- Deliveries consume named Leaves only when submitted.
- Rewards and unlocks are granted together; capacity failure leaves the task unclaimed.
- Up to three available tasks may be pinned.
- Pinning affects the tracker, not whether progress counts.
- The tracker can be collapsed and its entries open the Taskboard.
- The Taskboard does not pause the garden.

**Visual treatment**

- Left page uses coin, Leaf, land-expansion, Inventory-expansion, paper, and marker art as marginal illustrations.
- Right page resembles a tidy Guild register with a pin, a Ready seal, and a claimed reward line.

### Spread 14 · Garden controls and preferences · pages 27–28

**Tab:** Controls  
**Purpose:** Record only interactions verified in the running implementation.

**Left page — Operating the garden**

- Select a hex to inspect it.
- Drag or select Seeds/Spores, then choose a target.
- Drag living organisms into Inventory to harvest them.
- Drag Garden Tools and Blockers to move or retrieve them.
- Primary drag on empty ground pans; middle-button drag pans from anywhere.
- Mouse wheel, pinch, minus, and plus zoom; Fit frames the garden.
- One-finger touch pans and two-finger pinch adjusts the view.
- Inventory resizes from its left edge; its focused handle supports Left/Right Arrow keys.
- Escape closes supported surfaces.

**Right page — Preferences**

- Stage plantings before applying.
- Simplified hex fills.
- Keep Inspector in front of Grimoire.
- State what each option changes and, just as importantly, what rule it does not change.
- A short accessibility note: buttons and essential explanations must remain available to keyboard focus and cannot rely only on hover or animation.

**Visual treatment**

- Use small mouse, touch, key, Fit, and Inventory-edge diagrams arranged like a control plate.
- Avoid a conventional keyboard-shortcut grid because most actions are direct pointer interactions.

### Spread 15 · Using the books and saving · pages 29–30

**Tab:** Controls  
**Purpose:** Finish with operations the player may need when returning after time away.

**Left page — Using the books**

- Open each book from its garden icon; each remembers its own last spread.
- Tabs jump between chapters; margins, page controls, and Left/Right Arrow keys turn spreads.
- The tabletop, stationery, and book pan as one scene.
- Book controls zoom, rotate, fit, and reset; gold corners also rotate.
- Inkwell creates reusable field notes that can be edited, moved, tucked, or removed.
- Page markers save spreads and can be named, written on, moved, or removed.
- Notes and markers save with the garden and use no Inventory space.

**Right page — Saving and recovery**

- Browser autosave is local, not cloud storage.
- Save Game downloads a portable `.ccsave` containing the complete garden state.
- Load Game validates a compatible save before replacement.
- Portable saves can move between supported browsers or devices.
- Closed time is not simulated when a save is reopened.
- New garden replaces progress after confirmation; recommend making a portable backup first.

**Visual treatment**

- Left page shows the blue Field Manual and green Grimoire as a matching pair.
- Right page uses a small flow: browser autosave → portable save → validated load.
- End with a restrained colophon-style line rather than a second summary.

## Density and typography targets

- Normal instructional page: approximately 140–220 words plus one diagram, or 220–300 words with no major illustration.
- Reference-table page: no more than ten short rows.
- Main page title: reuse the current 32 px book title size.
- Body text: retain the current 15 px normal book size and line height.
- Small captions: retain 12–13 px, but do not place essential rules only in small text.
- Keep at least one visually quiet region on each page for notes and page markers to overlap without hiding the entire explanation.
- Avoid more than two heading levels on one page.
- Do not solve overflow by globally shrinking text. Move a paragraph, shorten wording, or add a spread if the normal-size review overflows.

## First implementation contract

The first in-game pass should provide:

1. A separate **Field Manual** launcher beneath Shop in the garden sidebar.
2. A blue book cover and a distinct launcher icon.
3. Five tabs with the order and labels in this document.
4. Fifteen stable spread keys and pages numbered 1–30.
5. Independent last-spread memory for the Field Manual and Grimoire.
6. Independent Field Manual notes and page markers while reusing the player's unlocked paper and marker styles.
7. The same page turning, tabletop movement, zoom, rotation, Fit, Reset, stationery, keyboard focus, and reduced-motion behavior as the Grimoire.
8. No changes to Grimoire chapters, entries, page numbers, discovery rules, or appearance.
9. No cross-book links in the first pass.
10. No provisional values, troubleshooting section, Transformation solutions, or hidden species information.

## Review after implementation

The first visual review should answer:

- Can every page be read at the same normal scale as the Grimoire without routine scrolling?
- Are the Condition, propagation, lifecycle, and Mycelial diagrams understandable without rereading the prose?
- Does the Blocker table remain legible when the whole spread is visible?
- Do five tabs look intentionally spaced rather than like half the tabs are missing?
- Is the blue launcher distinct from the Grimoire and Shop at ordinary sidebar size?
- Can the player switch between books without losing the place, view, notes, or markers in either one?
- Does opening the Field Manual preserve the live-garden interaction behavior already accepted for the Grimoire?

Only after that review should the plan add cross-navigation, troubleshooting, extra diagrams, or a different spread count.
